# Income Statement Analysis

**Project Overview**

**Understanding income statement analysis**

Think of your business like your personal finances. Every month money comes in from your job and goes out on rent, food and subscriptions. At the end of the month, you check; did I actually save anything? An income statement is actually that check. But for a company it is also called a Profit & Loss(P&L) statement, and it answers the most important question in business: is this company making or losing money?

 **Why This Matter?**
 
Imagine running a lemonade stand without ever counting your costs. You sell 100 cups, great! But if the lemons, cups and sugar cost more than charged, you’re actually loosing money with every sale. Analyzing an income statement stops exactly that from happening at a corporate scale. It tells a business where its winning, where its bleeding, and what to fix.

**Key Focus Area**

•	Which companies lead on net margin, operating margin and gross margin

•	How much profit they have left after all the expenses

•	How the business made profits overtime

•	How expenses affect the business

**Data Sources**

**About Data:**

This dataset contains standardized annual and quarterly income statements for various (23) publicly traded companies, it provides a granular look at corporate performance overtime.

**Key features include;**

•	Timeseries indicators: fiscal date ending & reported currency

•	Revenue metrics: total revenue, gross profit, & cost of revenue

•	Operating insights: operating income, operating expenses, research & development and selling general & administrative

•	Profitability ratios: EBIT, EBITDA, and net income

•	Tax & interest: income tax expenses, interest expenses and income before tax

 **conclusion & Recommendations:**

**Conclusion;**

Across these companies, a single pattern surfaces repeatedly; revenue growth is cheap, margin discipline is rare. The companies that mastered cost control did not just survive economic cycles; they compounded.

**TSM;**

Starting from a revenue base of $147B, the company scaled to approximately $2T by 2025, a trajectory that speaks not just to market dominance in semiconductor fabrication, but to structural pricing power few manufacturers ever achieve. The lone exception worth noting: a 2015 spike in the expense ratio placed a short-term pressure on profit margins. It was brief, corrected, and ultimately immaterial to the long arc of profitability.
What distinguishes TSM is not just the scale of revenue growth, but the consistency of profit expansion alongside it.

**WMT;**

Walmart presents a structurally challenging picture. Revenue has grown consistently, a testament to its retail scale and geographic reach. But revenue growth, in isolation, is a vanity metric. The more telling signal is a steadily rising expense ratio that has compressed profitability over time.
The number suggest a business that may be borrowing its growth, funding scale through cost base expansion rather than operational leverage.

**XOM;**

Exxon Mobil opened at a strong $200B + revenue base in 2005, the company generated adequate returns through much the decade. How ever in 2020 it hit its inflation point. Revenue collapsed to $178B, the expense ratio breached 113%, and profitability metrics turned deeply negative. The recovery by 2025, reflected a declining expense ratio and a return to positive margins.

**NVDA;**

NVDA wrestled with expenses management, but as at 2024, that ratio had compressed to 44% and as at 2025 to 47%. Revenue, profitability and net income all moved in lockstep upward through the final years of the analysis window.

**V;**

Visa demonstrates a level of consistency and efficiency. This business operates on an asset light, network effects driven model that naturally protects margins at scale. Every new card holder and merchant that enters its network adds revenue with minimal incremental cost. Amongst all the companies in this analysis, Visa had the highest gross, net and operating margin.

**JNJ;**

The company maintained consistent profitability across the full analysis period, with rational expenses ratio and a net income profile that held up under pressure. The complication: revenue contracted from $82B in 2020 to approximately $69B by 2025.

**Recommendations;**

**TSM – Taiwan semiconductor manufacturing company**

 Management should prioritize operational efficiency by implementing more rigorous cost controls across production and overhead functions.
 
**WMT- Walmart Inc**
.
The company should reduce its reliance on debt financed revenue generation and shift toward internally driven growth. A focus on streamlining the cost structure, in supply chain and operating expenses would at least improve profitability margins.

**XOM-ExxonMobil Corporation**

To strengthen its financial performance, the company should pursue targeted cost rationalized efforts, with particular attention to eliminating non-essential expenditures across its operations.

**NVDA- NVIDIA Corporation**

While the company demonstrated commendable expense discipline, reducing its operating expense ratio from approximately 89% to 44% between prior periods. The recent uptick to 47% in fiscal year 2025 warrants attention. Management should reinstate tighter cost controls to prevent expense ratios from eroding profit margins. Sustaining the gains achieved will require proactive monitoring of cost growth relative to revenue scaling.

**JNJ- Johnson & Johnson**

The company should undertake a structured review of its operating cost base with the objective of achieving sustainable expense reduction. Targeting inefficiencies across its business segments, without compromising its R&D pipeline or core operations, would meaningfully improve net profitability and strengthen its long-term financial position.

**V- Visa Inc.**

Management should focus on diversifying and growing its revenue streams to reduce dependance on existing income sources, coupled with enhanced expense management, these measures would position the company for more resilient and consistent earning growth.
